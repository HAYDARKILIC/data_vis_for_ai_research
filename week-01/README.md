# Week 1 — Scientific data types, magnitudes, and distributions

> Exploratory analysis of distributions, proportions, and variable relationships;
> presentation ethics, and a critical look at how AI model outputs are visualized.

## Learning objectives

By the end of this week you should be able to:

1. Identify the **measurement type** of a variable (nominal, ordinal, interval, ratio)
   and explain why it constrains the encoding choices available to you.
2. Choose between linear, log, and symlog scales for distributions whose magnitudes span
   many orders of magnitude — a regular case in modern AI (loss values, parameter counts,
   token frequencies).
3. Read and produce conditional-distribution plots: violin, strip, swarm, ECDF, and
   ridgeline, and argue when each is more honest than a bar of means.
4. Spot the most common dishonest patterns in AI papers and dashboards — truncated axes,
   averaged-away variance, cherry-picked seeds, and chart-junk obscuring an effect that
   is actually small.

## Suggested reading

- Wilke, *Fundamentals of Data Visualization*, chs. 6–10. Open-access at clauswilke.com.
- Cleveland & McGill (1984), "Graphical Perception", *JASA*. The original ranking of
  visual encodings.
- Cairo (2019), *How Charts Lie*. Especially chapter 3 on truncated axes.
